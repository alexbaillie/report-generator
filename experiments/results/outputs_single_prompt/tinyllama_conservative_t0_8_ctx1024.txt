A cautious clinical psychology assistant has conducted an assessment of a 7-year-old child referred for concerns related to attention, impulsivity, and academic performance. The child's parents reported difficulties sustaining attention during classroom tasks, frequent distractibility at home, and inconsistent task completion scores. Assessments with the child revealed average intellectual functioning, although working memory and processing speed were weaker compared to her peers. The child has been observed exhibiting inconsistent task completion and engaging in challenging multi-step instructions, but behavioral rating scales indicate mildly elevated scores in the areas of attention and executive functioning. Cognitive testing showed overall average intellectual functioning, with strengths noted in verbal comprehension. No significant emotional or behavioral concerns were reported beyond attention-related difficulties. Assessment results indicate that caution should be taken when making diagnoses or making causal explanations regarding the child's performance on assessments and tasks.

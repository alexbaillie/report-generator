Title: Child with Weak Working Memory Impacts Academic Functioning: A Cognitive and Attention-Related Summary of Intake Notes and Test Results

INTRODUCTION:
The child referred to in the intake notes is a 9-year-old boy who has been experiencing difficulties sustaining attention and completing tasks. The teacher reports difficulty with attentional control, which may be impacting academic performance. The child's test results indicate that he has weak working memory, as measured by the WIsc-V (Wechsler Intelligence Scale for Children-IV) and the WMi (Working Memory Index).

Cognitive Summary:
The child's cognitive abilities are generally above average, with strong verbal and visual memory skills. However, his working memory is weak, which may impact his ability to sustain attention and complete tasks effectively. The WIsc-V measures the child's ability to maintain focus on a task for a set period of time, while the WMi assesses the child's ability to recall and manipulate information quickly and accurately.

Attention-Related Summary:
The child's attention is generally good, with no significant difficulties in sustaining focus or switching between tasks. However, his working memory may be impaired, which could impact his ability to complete tasks effectively. The WIsc-V measures the child's ability to maintain focused attention on a task for a set period of time, while the WMi assesses the child's ability to recall and manipulate information quickly and accurately.

Conclusion:
The child's cognitive and attention-related abilities are generally above average, but his working memory is weak, which may impact his academic performance. The WIsc-V measures the child's ability to maintain focused attention on a task for a set period of time, while the WMi assesses the child's ability to recall and manipulate information quickly and accurately. It is recommended that the child receive additional support in areas such as executive functioning and self-regulation to improve his overall academic performance.
